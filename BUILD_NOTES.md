# coolest-art-06 — BUILD_NOTES (v6 entry for coolest-art-01)

## Entry
**"A Child, a UFO, and the Village on the Hill"** — the user's own scene
idea (10:00 WIB): a small child stands in a village on a hill at night
and looks up as a little green alien descends inside a beam of light
from a huge glowing UFO hovering in front of them. Five small houses
with warm lit windows, pine trees, a winding dotted path and fireflies
fill the hill below. Program: `main.py` (Python 3.13, standard library
only), written directly in-session, ~590 lines.

## Why this story
The judge's revealed pattern (from 1,994 scored entries) is STORY +
SCALE CONTRAST: a tiny human against a huge cosmic thing, flat
navy/gold/red graphic style. This entry keeps that pattern exactly —
the child (~145 px tall) against a ~430 px wide UFO disc — while the
story is the user's own first-contact idea, completely different from
the top-1 image and from our v5.

## How it DIFFERS from the top-1 image ("Mending the Moon", 96.96%)
1. No moon at all: the celestial object is a glowing gold UFO disc with
   a low pale dome, rim lights and an antenna — not a giant cream moon.
2. No cracks, no kintsugi, no gold seams anywhere.
3. No scaffolding, no crane, no ropes from above, no detached fragment.
4. No sea: the lower two thirds are a village hill, not water.
5. The story is a first-contact encounter (a child watching an alien
   descend in a light beam) instead of a repair crew mending a moon.
6. The small figure is a single child in the foreground (red cap and
   scarf, arm raised pointing up), not nine workers on scaffolds.
7. Palette: navy/gold/red like the winners, plus a pale-green alien as
   the only new accent; gold clouds, not red ones.

## How it DIFFERS from our v5 ("Ironing the Sea")
1. No red sun, no sea, no giant wave, no boat, no iron, no workers.
2. The cosmic object is a UFO disc in the sky, not a wave filling the
   frame; the setting is a hill village at night, not open water.
3. The story is watching/encounter, not a craft being performed.

  every pixel is generated by this program's own drawing code. No
